cutlist

Sheet goods cut optimizer. Use any unit (mm or inches) — just be consistent.

How to use

1. Enter what you have and what you need

  • Sheet goods: one row per material, with its size, how many sheets you have, the edge trim (the strip cut off each edge to square it up; 0 for none) and a colour for the diagrams. Set the blade kerf (saw blade thickness).
  • Panels: label, length, width, quantity and material for each part to cut. Panels may be turned 90° to fit; turned ones are marked ↻.
  • Number fields accept arithmetic, e.g. =600-2*18. Drag the ⠿ handle to reorder rows.

2. Optimize

  • Pick a Layout: rip first (full-length strips), crosscut first (full-width strips) or best fit, then click Optimize.
  • Each sheet shows its layout with numbered red cut lines in saw order, and a cut list below: Set at is the fence setting (width cut off). Show cuts hides or shows them.
  • Export PDF saves the inputs and layouts (as shown on screen) for the workshop.

3. Measure on a diagram

  • Click near a corner to start measuring, move to another point to read the distance, click again (or Esc) to clear. The ends snap to panel and sheet corners and to where cut lines meet.
  • Hold Shift for a horizontal or vertical measurement; it also snaps to the edge trim lines.

4. Rearrange by hand

  • Drag a panel to another place on its sheet; the others move to make room. Dark grey means it can't go there: dropping puts it back. Esc cancels.
  • While dragging, R turns the panel 90° and P parks it under the sheet, out of the way, to rearrange the rest. Drag parked panels back onto the sheet; they are not in the cut list while parked.
  • Clicking Optimize again replaces your arrangement.

5. Save

  • Name the project and click Save; open it later from the Open list. A hand-made arrangement is saved too, and shown again while the inputs are unchanged.
